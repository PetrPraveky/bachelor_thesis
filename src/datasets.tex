\section{Mapping Frameworks Verification}

Four mapping frameworks that support \ac{ROS2} were chosen: OctoMap~\cite{bk_octomap}, WaveMap~\cite{bk_wavemap}, RayFronts~\cite{bk_rayfronts} and volumetric/occupancy-based mapper~\cite{bk_capek_masters}. The selected mapping frameworks differ in their primary purpose, but they partially overlap in the supported map representations. The overlap is mainly visible in semantic mapping, \ac{ESDF} based representations, and occupancy mapping. On \reftab{tab:supported_mapping_features} is shown which features are supported by which mapping frameworks.

\begin{table*}[!h]
	\centering
	\noindent
	\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
		Mapping Framework & Semantic mapping & \ac{ESDF} & Occupancy mapping \\
		\hline
		\makecell{Volumetric/ \\ occupancy-based mapper} & Yes & Yes & Yes \\
		OctoMap & No & No & Yes \\
		WaveMap & No & Yes & No \\
		RayFronts & Yes & No & No
	\end{tabular}
	\noindent
	\caption{Table of mapping frameworks supported features.}
	\label{tab:supported_mapping_features}
\end{table*}

In general, mapping frameworks are often evaluated on datasets that do not perfectly correspond to real-world conditions. For this reason, the selected mapping frameworks were evaluated using the datasets created in this thesis to demonstrate their behavior when processing large outdoor environments. The observed metrics were point cloud insertion time, memory consumption, and CPU usage. These metrics are visualized over time in Figs.~\ref{fig:insertion_time}--\ref{fig:cpu_usage} using datasets with IDs 0102, 0201, 0302 and 0402.
